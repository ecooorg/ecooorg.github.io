TopFlameless Heating Boiler

Autonomous flameless thermochemical heating boiler with gravity feed of encapsulated composite metal fuel briquettes, passive bimetallic ash removal and in-situ catalytic hydrogen recombination

ZenodoTDCommons

1. TECHNICAL FIELD

The invention relates to autonomous heating systems for residential and small commercial buildings, specifically to flameless thermochemical boilers utilizing controlled exothermic reactions between a solid composite metal-based fuel, water vapour and atmospheric oxygen. The boiler is classified under IPC classes F24H 1/00, F24V 30/00, C01B 3/08, F23C 13/00 and C10L 5/40. It represents a combined system for heat generation and a specialised solid composite fuel manufactured from processed industrial waste. Once initiated, the boiler operates passively in a self-sustained mode and requires only minimal electricity during a brief start-up phase.

2. BACKGROUND ART

Biomass pellet boilers operating according to the EN 303-5 standard are widely used in residential heating. While functional, these systems depend heavily on continuous mechanical operation of motorised screw feeders and active ash-removal assemblies. Biomass pellets are inherently hygroscopic; exposure to ambient moisture causes them to swell and frequently jam the feeding mechanisms. In addition, the combustion of organic pellets generates measurable emissions of carbon dioxide (CO₂), carbon monoxide (CO) and fine dust, making them subject to increasing carbon taxation across European jurisdictions.

Thermochemical heat generation methods based on pure metals are also known. The classic steam-iron process using pure iron requires operational temperatures exceeding 500 °C, which is difficult to manage safely in a domestic installation. Systems based on pure aluminium powder present severe dust-explosion hazards regulated under ATEX directives. Alternative hydrogen-based heating designs that store or transport the gas under pressure introduce significant explosion risks inside living spaces due to possible leakage and gas accumulation.

The closest prior-art solutions utilise replaceable multi-cell cartridges filled with reactive metal mixtures. These configurations require the end-user to manually replace heavy spent modules several times during a single heating season. Furthermore, the unprotected metal composite within these cartridges undergoes slow premature oxidation if ambient moisture penetrates the storage enclosure prior to use.

3. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The objective of the present invention is to provide a mechanically simple, safe and convenient flameless heating boiler that can be loaded with a full season’s fuel supply in a single operation, requires no external electrical power after start-up, and operates with zero carbon emissions.

The technical solution is achieved through the following interconnected elements:

A. Encapsulated Composite Fuel Briquettes

The fuel is supplied as pre-compressed porous blocks weighing between 2.5 and 3.5 kg. The composition consists by mass of 75–80 % shredded iron shavings, 10–12 % recycled aluminium or magnesium particles, and 8–10 % calcium oxide (CaO). Each briquette is fully sealed within a continuous moisture-proof protective layer 0.3–1.0 mm thick, composed of technical paraffin or a low-temperature polymer. This layer undergoes clean thermal liquefaction and gasification without leaving solid carbonaceous residues in the temperature range of 150 °C to 250 °C.

B. Passive Gravity-Fed Storage Magazine

The boiler incorporates a vertical or inclined unmotorised storage shaft dimensioned to contain the fuel volume for an entire heating season. The internal surfaces of the shaft are smooth to reduce friction. The shaft transitions at its lower end into a thermal pre-chamber adjacent to the primary reaction chamber. No active cutters, mechanical knives or electrical de-encapsulation devices are used. When the lowest briquette descends into the thermal pre-chamber, the protective coating liquefies and drains away into an isolated collector, thereby exposing the porous core to water vapour.

C. In-Situ Hydrogen Recombination Matrix

The primary reaction chamber is partitioned into an array of isolated micro-cells, each having a maximum internal volume of 80 ml. This volumetric limitation serves as a passive safety measure against gas accumulation. Positioned directly above each micro-cell is a porous ceramic matrix coated with transition metal oxides, in particular copper and manganese oxides. Hydrogen generated by the iron-steam reaction passes through the matrix and is instantly oxidised by atmospheric air in a flameless catalytic reaction. Free hydrogen is consumed within milliseconds of its formation.

D. Passive Bimetallic Ash-Removal Mechanism

The lower boundary of each micro-cell is formed by a movable grid supported by passive bimetallic plates made of two metals with different coefficients of thermal expansion. Temperature fluctuations within the operating range of 280 °C to 420 °C cause these plates to undergo sudden snapping deformations. The mechanical action shatters the brittle magnetite (Fe₃O₄) crust forming on the briquette surface, allowing the spent oxide to fall by gravity into a lower collection bunker.

E. Auxiliary Electrical Start-Up and Safety Fuse

A low-power ceramic heating element (100–250 W) is located near the thermal pre-chamber. It operates exclusively during the initial cold start to raise the local temperature to 180–220 °C and is then permanently deactivated. The capillary water supply system is fitted with a passive safety fuse consisting of a low-melting-point alloy gate. If the temperature inside any reaction cell exceeds 450 °C, the alloy melts and permanently seals the water channels, terminating the reaction.

4. DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

Although the present application is filed without drawings, the following description is sufficiently detailed to enable a person skilled in the art to carry out the invention.

The boiler comprises a heavily insulated vertical metallic body surrounded by a water jacket connected to the building’s hydronic heating circuit. At the beginning of the heating season the storage shaft is filled with the required number of encapsulated composite briquettes, which stack under their own weight.

To start the system from a cold state, the auxiliary ceramic heater (typically 150 W) is energised for 5 to 10 minutes until the temperature in the lower part of the thermal pre-chamber reaches approximately 200 °C. At this temperature the protective coating of the lowest briquette decomposes cleanly. Water is then introduced through a passive gravity-syphon system operating under negative pressure.

The rapid hydration of calcium oxide (CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + 65 kJ/mol) together with the hydrolysis of the aluminium particles produces a quick thermal upsurge. This raises the temperature of the main iron matrix into the autothermal range of 280 °C to 420 °C, after which the electrical heater is switched off and the boiler continues in fully autonomous mode.

Inside the micro-cells, water vapour reacts with the iron shavings to form magnetite and hydrogen. The hydrogen rises through the porous catalytic matrix, mixes with atmospheric oxygen and recombines flamelessly, releasing the primary heat to the water jacket. Condensate returns to the water-feed loop, keeping water consumption low.

As the reaction proceeds, the cyclic injection of water and the resulting heat generation create local temperature fluctuations. Every 20 to 40 minutes these fluctuations cause the bimetallic plates to snap, dislodging the spent magnetite crust. The broken oxide falls through the grid into the ash bunker. When the lower briquette is consumed and shortens, the next briquette drops by gravity into the active zone.

If the temperature inside any reaction cell exceeds 450 °C, the low-melting-point alloy fuse melts and permanently closes the water channels, stopping the thermochemical reaction.

5. CLAIMS

An autonomous flameless thermochemical heating boiler comprising a housing with a water jacket connected to a building heating circuit, a reaction chamber linked to a water supply system, and means for transferring heat to the water jacket, characterized in that:

the boiler is provided with an unmotorized gravity-fed storage magazine arranged for vertical or inclined storage and purely gravity-driven delivery of solid composite metal fuel briquettes having a mass of 2.5 to 3.5 kg each;

each fuel briquette is covered with a hermetic, moisture-proof, thermally degradable protective layer based on technical paraffin and/or a low-temperature polymer configured to melt or decompose without leaving solid residues in the temperature range of 150 °C to 250 °C;

the reaction chamber is internally divided into an array of isolated micro-cells each having a volume of not more than 80 ml, wherein each micro-cell is covered from above by an integrated porous catalytic matrix containing transition metal oxides configured to perform instant in-situ flameless recombination of generated hydrogen with atmospheric oxygen directly inside the cell;

and a passive ash-removal arrangement is provided at the bottom of the micro-cells, the arrangement comprising passive bimetallic structural elements configured to undergo cyclic snapping deformations triggered by temperature changes in the operating range of 280 °C to 420 °C, thereby dislodging spent iron oxide (Fe₃O₄) into a lower collection bunker.

The heating boiler according to claim 1, characterized in that the solid composite metal fuel briquettes have a controlled internal porosity and consist by mass of 75–80 % shredded iron shavings, 10–12 % recycled aluminium or magnesium particles, and 8–10 % calcium oxide (CaO).

The heating boiler according to claim 1, characterized in that the porous catalytic matrix is coated with an active layer of copper oxides and/or manganese oxides applied by liquid chemical deposition followed by thermal pyrolysis.

The heating boiler according to claim 1, characterized in that it further comprises an auxiliary ceramic electrical heating element of 100 to 250 W capacity arranged near the thermal pre-chamber and configured to operate exclusively during cold start until a temperature of 180 °C to 220 °C is reached.

The heating boiler according to claim 1, characterized in that the water supply system is a passive gravity-syphon arrangement operating under negative pressure and is fitted with a passive thermomechanical safety fuse made of a low-melting-point alloy that irreversibly closes the water channels if the temperature inside the reaction chamber exceeds 450 °C.

6. ABSTRACT

The invention relates to an autonomous flameless thermochemical heating boiler operating on composite metal fuel briquettes (iron–aluminium–calcium oxide) with a unit mass of approximately 3 kg. The briquettes are protected by a thin paraffin- or polymer-based coating that prevents moisture penetration during long-term storage and decomposes automatically upon entering a hot thermal pre-chamber. Fuel sufficient for an entire heating season is loaded into a vertical gravity-fed shaft once a year. Hydrogen produced during the metal-steam reaction is immediately recombined on porous catalytic matrices, preventing free gas accumulation. Spent iron oxide is removed by an unpowered bimetallic snapping grid. After a brief electrical start-up phase the boiler operates in a self-sustaining mode with zero carbon dioxide emissions.
