A Load-Bearing, Fluid-Mediated Robotic Joint Architecture Combining Variable-Geometry Friction Interlocks with Passive Multi-Stage Granular Jamming Inside a Tensegrity Compression Frame

ZenodoTDCommons

1. Theoretical Foundation and Document Scope

This technical disclosure specifies the mechanical architecture, boundary mechanics, and control parameters of the QUINTO anthropomorphic joint system. The data, sizing curves, and structural calculations in this document come from multi-domain physical simulation, CAD geometric modeling, and finite element analysis (FEA). As of September 14, 2026, no material prototyping or empirical bench testing has been completed. This text is entered into the public domain to establish defensive prior art, so that the structural assemblies and operational sequences described here cannot later be claimed under patent applications filed by other parties.

Technical Abstract

This paper describes the QUINTO-CMC modular articulation architecture, built to hold high structural loads in contaminated or corrosive operating environments while cutting static holding power to a minimal telemetry-only draw. The system replaces high-maintenance, actively powered joints with an unpowered, field-replaceable cartridge that locks mechanically under zero electrical load. The physical joint module weighs no more than 1.75 kg. Static holding draw is limited to 18–25 W for telemetry and monitoring alone — the mechanical lock itself needs no electrical power. Three kinematically uncoupled passive locking layers trigger in sequence during pressure drops or sudden fluid loss. The primary retention tier uses a 4.5° self-locking mechanical wedge gate, where laser-etched micro-surfaces hold an operational friction coefficient of at least 0.11 nominal (0.095 as a conservative end-of-life floor) when submerged in a colloidal magnetic nanoparticle medium. The auxiliary tier deploys a granular particle-jamming core that triggers when system pressure drops below 3.0 MPa, giving an unpowered structural lock that stays disengaged through normal transient pressure dips down to 2.1 MPa. A radial friction stop forms the final safety boundary, limiting structural backlash to 0.5°. Hydraulic fluid routing runs through the internal hollow passages of the rigid compression struts in a structural tensegrity frame, keeping the high-pressure working fluid circuits fully separated from the dry tension network. This document records these mechanism combinations as prior art, protecting the disclosed technical baseline from third-party patent claims.

2. Engineering Requirements and Governing Constraints

The technical requirements below set the design boundaries of the joint module. Each functions as an independent constraint that must hold across all operational configurations.

  • REQ_INV_ST_SEAL_1 (Fluid Retention Boundary): Internal hydraulic fluid leakage must stay at or below 0.005 mL/min under a continuous operating pressure of 12 MPa, with an analytical upper safety ceiling mapped to 15 MPa.
  • REQ_INV_FAIL_SAFE_1 (Multi-Tier Passive Lock): The joint core must support a zero-power structural holding state through automatic, unpowered engagement of a primary mechanical wedge, an auxiliary pressure-drop granular jamming block, and a radial friction end-stop.
  • REQ_INV_MAINTAIN_1 (Cartridge Field Interchange): Interface dimensions and mechanical alignments must allow a complete field exchange of the preassembled cartridge module within 4 minutes, using standard mechanical or pneumatic toggle clamps and no specialized depot tools.
  • REQ_INV_MASS_1 (Knee Module Mass Envelope): Total mass of the assembled joint module must not exceed 1.75 kg, including internal actuator frames, integrated pump elements, fluids, and diagnostic telemetry electronics. See Section 6 for the baseline and optional-module mass budgets.
  • REQ_INV_SENS_1 (Local Interrogation Rate): Local sensor arrays monitoring the fluid boundary must support a telemetry query and signal update frequency of at least 500 Hz, resolving transient pressure anomalies before valve closure.
  • REQ_INV_THERM_1 (Thermal Core Envelope): Material profiles and fluid properties must stay stable across an environmental thermal range of –20°C to +75°C, supported by localized software limits and auxiliary heating grids.
  • REQ_INV_SELF_LOCK_MARGIN_1 (Deterministic Stability Margin): The primary mechanical lock must maintain a geometric-tribological safety index tan(alpha)/mu ≤ 0.90 across all cumulative manufacturing tolerances (±0.1° on the wedge angle), thermal drift, and surface wear. The ratio is evaluated against the conservative end-of-life friction floor mu = 0.095 defined in Section 4.1, not the nominal fresh-surface value of 0.11, so the margin holds over the cartridge's full service life.

Trade-offs and Limitations

The figures above are not unconditional advantages. The QUINTO architecture gives up active back-drivability and fine torque-control precision available in electric joints, since the wedge and granular locks are intentionally non-backdrivable once engaged. It also demands tighter manufacturing tolerances (±0.1° on the wedge half-angle, controlled femtosecond-laser texturing) than either reference architecture, and the consumable-cartridge strategy shifts recurring cost from field labor to replacement-part logistics — which may not suit every deployment context.

Methodology note: the static holding load and dynamic power figures assume an ambient temperature of 20°C, a wedge surface at the nominal 4.5° angle with mu = 0.11, and a structural safety factor of 1.4 from linear-elastic FEA of the titanium-alloy frame. None of these values have been empirically validated (see Section 1); treat them as design targets pending physical testing.

4. Mechanical Principles of the Three-Stage Passive Lock

Joint holding safety rests on three mechanical constraints arranged to engage in sequence along distinct kinematic axes, without continuous sensor oversight or external power.

An axial force applied to the joint first meets the primary wedge gate, where the self-locking condition tan(alpha) < mu (alpha = 4.5°) holds the joint in place with no power input. If local hydraulic pressure falls below 3.0 MPa — the signature of a leak or line rupture rather than a normal transient — the granular chamber described in Section 4.2 undergoes a phase transition and locks its movement volume. This second stage acts on a decoupled, independent radial displacement path, so it cannot bind against the primary wedge. A friction end-stop closes the sequence, holding mechanical backlash at or below 0.5° regardless of which of the first two stages is carrying load.

4.1 Primary Wedge Gate Geometry and Interface Tribology

The first locking tier uses a taper wedge gate layout built to satisfy the self-braking equation tan(alpha) < mu. The angle is fixed at 4.5°; manufacturing variance allows a maximum drift of ±0.1°, capping the working angle at 4.6°. A suspension fluid carrying functional magnetic nanoparticles creates a boundary lubrication state across the primary friction surfaces, where contact forces are carried directly by microscopic surface asperities.

Two friction coefficients bound the design, and they do not conflict: a nominal value of mu = 0.11 on freshly textured surfaces under calibrated fluid-film conditions, and a conservative end-of-life floor of mu = 0.095 after cumulative wear, fluid-film buildup, and nanoparticle agglomeration over the cartridge's service life. REQ_INV_SELF_LOCK_MARGIN_1 is checked against this worst-case floor, not the nominal value: at the maximum manufacturing angle of 4.6°, tan(alpha) ≈ 0.0804, so tan(alpha)/mu ≈ 0.0804 / 0.095 ≈ 0.85.

At the nominal 0.11 value the same ratio comes out to ≈ 0.73, a wider margin — which is why the 0.095 floor is the governing case.

The femtosecond-laser cross-hatched texture keeps in-service friction near the nominal 0.11 value by draining carrier fluid under load and forcing direct asperity interlocking. The 0.095 floor is kept only as a design margin against wear over the full service interval and is not expected to be reached in normal operation. This limits total friction-coefficient degradation to no more than 0.015 — a drop from 0.11 nominal to the 0.095 floor — across the cartridge's lifecycle, and prevents back-driving under full load.

4.2 Secondary Particle-Jamming Core and Trigger Mechanics

An auxiliary chamber holding rigid granular media provides a secondary passive lock in parallel with the primary wedge. An unpowered spring-biased actuator compresses the granular fill once local hydraulic pressure drops below 3.0 MPa. That threshold is set to avoid accidental locking during rapid deceleration or direction changes: computational fluid analysis shows that high-velocity joint reversals produce local fluid inertia and transient pressure drops as low as 2.1 MPa, so the 3.0 MPa trigger leaves a 0.9 MPa security buffer. The granular core stays loose and fluid during aggressive dynamic maneuvers, then transitions to a rigid solid structure during actual fluid loss or a line rupture. Full mechanical lock-up, from the pressure crossing 3.0 MPa to the granular core reaching its jammed state, completes within the same 3–5 ms valve-closure window used by the optional hydro-mechanical damping package (Section 5), since that package's micro-throttle labyrinth governs how fast pressure can fall past the trigger point in the first place. An inductive monitoring loop tracks shifts in internal void spaces to record grain wear and settling over time.

4.3 Tertiary Decoupled Friction Stop

The third tier is a continuous radial friction wedge that caps final positioning backlash at 0.5° or less. It is kinematically isolated from the primary axial wedge, moving along a separate radial displacement axis. This mechanical decoupling removes structural over-constraint and keeps the two locking mechanisms from jamming or binding against each other in normal operation.

5. Integrated Cartridge Architecture and Severe Environment Profiles

The system groups the locking wedge, graded polymer-to-metal sealing tracks, the granular jamming core, and local telemetry coils into a single monolithic cartridge block. Baseline block mass is 0.080 kg. Fitting the optional hydro-mechanical damping module — a micro-throttle labyrinth and disc spring stack that caps fluid pressure spikes at 12 MPa by holding valve closure times inside a 3–5 ms window — raises the total cartridge assembly mass from 0.080 kg to 0.095 kg (including the 0.015 kg damping module as broken down in Table 6.1), using 0.015 kg of the joint's internal mass margin. The damping module is optional; Section 6 reports the mass budget both without it and with it installed. Three severe operational cases below demonstrate enablement.

  • High-Radiation Industrial Cells: Rated for total ionizing radiation doses up to 50 kGy. The cartridge housing is a titanium structure built by binder jetting, paired with radiation-stable nitrile elastomer seals. The joint holds heavy protective shielding walls for extended durations at zero power. The 4-minute field swap profile lets remote handling claws change the cartridge without manual human intervention inside the hot cell.
  • Deep-Sea Marine Systems: Configured for an external hydrostatic pressure of 40 MPa in highly corrosive saltwater. The main joint structure includes an automated pneumatic clearing line and a sliding mechanical guard shutter, which blows a compressed air burst across the mating faces immediately before docking to purge ocean water from the seal seats. The internal magnetic fluid is pressure-compensated to keep seawater from migrating across the primary sealing line, preserving the 0.005 mL/min leakage limit.
  • Hyper-Arid Logistics Operations: Built for continuous low-frequency vibration (10 Hz to 500 Hz at 3.5 g RMS) in environments reaching +75°C ambient with fine silica dust. Laser-etched open micro-gutters on the wedge face push airborne dust particles out of the contact zone on each cycle. To stop vibration-induced settling in the granular chamber, low-power 32 kHz ultrasonic crystals embedded in the outer frame run brief vibration cycles when unladen, clearing loose grain clusters.

7. System Energy and Power Constraints

Static Holding Power: total continuous electrical draw is capped at 18–25 W, covering only the local data buses and inductive telemetry coils. The mechanical lock itself holds full design load at zero electrical power, consistent with REQ_INV_FAIL_SAFE_1.

Dynamic Operating Bandwidth: power demand spans 170 W to 360 W during peak joint movements and high-flow fluid acceleration loops (design target; see the methodology note in Section 3).

8. Defensive Patent Definitions and Structural Combinations

The structural combinations below define the core inventive concepts disclosed in this document, establishing public prior art:

  • System Architecture Combination (Definition 1): A modular robotic joint comprising a monolithic multi-material consumable cartridge integrated with a secondary granular particle-jamming loop that engages passively, within the same 3–5 ms valve-closing window as the internal micro-throttle damping labyrinth, when local system pressure drops below 3.0 MPa, combined with that micro-throttle damping labyrinth limiting fluid shock to 12 MPa across the same window.
  • Mechanical Interface (Definition 2): A mechanical retention structure comprising a self-locking wedge gate with a nominal angle of 4.5° ± 0.1° that satisfies a structural holding ratio of tan(alpha)/mu ≤ 0.90 evaluated at a conservative end-of-life friction floor of mu ≥ 0.095, where the contacting surfaces contain open laser-etched fluid channels maintaining a nominal boundary friction coefficient of mu ≥ 0.11 when submerged in a colloidal magnetic nanoparticle medium, tracked via an embedded 500 Hz inductive telemetry link.
  • Fluid Routing and Layout (Definition 3): A robotic limb assembly using a structural tensegrity framework, where high-pressure hydraulic conduits run entirely within the internal hollow volumes of rigid, non-deformable compression struts, maintaining complete spatial separation from dry, unpenetrated tension elements.
